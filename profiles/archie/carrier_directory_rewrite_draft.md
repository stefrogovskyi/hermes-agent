A shipping line's network rarely fits on one screen; checking a route usually means digging through separate carrier websites, port lists, and sailing schedules just to piece the picture together. Carrier Directory puts that scattered picture back together: search by route, or start from a carrier and see where it actually sails.

Use it to identify which carriers serve a given origin and destination, check a shipping line's port network, open detailed sea and dry port pages, and move on to freight rates, container tracking, ship schedules, fleet information, or demurrage and storage figures for whatever you find.

## Getting into Carrier Directory

Open References in the SeaRates menu, then choose Carrier Directory. The same References section holds World Sea Ports, the Demurrage & Storage Calculator, and a handful of other shipping references worth knowing about.

Two tabs split the database in half. By Route surfaces shipping lines active between two locations. By Carrier flips the question around: pick a line first, then see its ports.

## Searching by route

When the locations are set but the carriers aren't, this is the tab to open.

Type the origin into the first field, the destination into the second, and select both from the suggestion list. That gives you a fixed point-to-point search, handy for a new freight request, a check on an existing route, or confirming who actually connects two ports. Hit the search button and the matching shipping lines load below.

Scroll the results and you already get useful numbers before clicking anything: how many ports a carrier operates, and its transit time on your specific route.

Pick Evergreen, say, on a Shanghai-Hamburg lane, and you land on its full profile inside the Shipping lines directory. From there:

- general carrier details, plus a live world map of its ports;
- real-time freight rates with tariffs guaranteed by the carrier, each one clickable through to Logistics Explorer for instant booking;
- container tracking further down the page, for shipments already moving with that line;
- fleet details, vessel locations where available, and sailing schedules with live deadlines through Ship Schedules;
- a demurrage, detention, and storage calculator, so free days at a given port don't come as a surprise.

## Searching by shipping line

Already know the carrier and just need its footprint? Open By Carrier instead.

Choose a line from the list, or search for it directly, and Carrier Directory pulls up the operational coverage tied to that carrier. The overview that follows breaks out two totals: how many sea ports and how many dry ports the chosen line calls at.

Narrow it down by country next. This is where you'd check whether a carrier shows up in a specific market, scout possible gateways, or work out the inland and maritime legs of a shipment before committing to it. Selecting a country filters the port list down to what that line actually calls there.

Each port card carries a handful of operational details before you even open it: port name, TEU figures, maximum depth, port type, and port size, enough to spot the right location without paging through every result.

Open a port card and the fuller record lays out where it's located, which authority runs it, its UN/LOCODE, the terminals on site, its physical and operational specs, and the carrier connections tied to that location. UN/LOCODE matters here mainly because so many ports share near-identical names, and it's worth having correct in a freight request, schedule, or shipping document. From the port page, a link through to World Sea Ports gives the complete record, one you can copy and forward straight to a customer, carrier, port operator, or partner.

## Route first or carrier first?

Two different starting points, two different questions. By Route fits a corridor you already have in mind, say a lane from Istanbul to Boston, when what you need is which lines actually run it. Flip that around for By Carrier: pick the line first, then check the matching country and port entries to see whether it touches Türkiye, the United States, or wherever else you're checking.

### A workflow that ties it together

Most logistics teams end up running some version of this sequence:

1. Search shipping lines by route.
2. Filter and review the carrier results.
3. Open the sealine page that fits.
4. Dig into its ports or fleet if the shipment needs it.
5. Check Ship Schedules for sailings.
6. Move to Logistics Explorer for freight options.
7. Track the shipment in Container Tracking once references come through.
8. Confirm Demurrage & Storage terms for that carrier and port.

Carrier research doesn't have to end at the profile page. It stays connected to booking, tracking, and cost-planning through the rest of SeaRates' tools.
