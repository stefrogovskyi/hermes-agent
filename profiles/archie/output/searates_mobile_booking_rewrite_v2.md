# Running Freight Bookings From Your Phone With SeaRates

**Meta title:** Manage Freight Bookings On the Go: SeaRates App
**Meta description:** See how SeaRates Mobile puts booking status, cargo details, contacts, and transport data in one app for freight teams working outside the office.

---

Freight work doesn't always happen behind a desk. Teams need to check on a booking's status between calls, confirm shipment details with a partner, look up route information, pull up a contact, or update transport data without stopping to find a desktop first.

SeaRates, a digital freight platform, built its Booking System into the SeaRates Mobile app, a mobile freight app available on iOS and Android, for exactly this kind of on-the-go logistics work. Freight bookings, shipment routes, booking details, real-time timeline updates, parties and contacts, transport information: it all lives in one freight booking app now instead of being spread across email threads, spreadsheets, and messengers.

## What's Actually Inside the Mobile Booking System

It's built for the people who touch bookings every day: shipment coordinators, freight forwarders, logistics providers, shippers, and anyone else on an operational team who needs booking data without opening a laptop. Think of it as a mobile freight workspace holding a shipment's numbers and routes, its current status, cargo and container specifics, who's carrying it, the people connected to it, an activity log, transport records, paperwork, finance details, and operational notes, all in one screen.

## Step 1. Open the Bookings List

Open Tools, then Booking System, and every active booking shows up as a list. Each card is a snapshot, enough to know what a shipment is about before tapping in for the full picture.

A booking card typically shows the booking number, the route (Mexico City, MX to Texas City, US, for instance), shipment type such as LAND_FCL ST20, RAIL_FTL TT90, FCL 20' Standard, or LCL, carrier status like Carrier TBN, a status flag like New, the profiles tied to that shipment, and the freight amount in USD. It covers multimodal shipments, sea, land, rail, FCL and LCL alike, without needing a separate screen for each mode.

## Step 2. Search and Filter Fast

When a team is juggling dozens of shipments, scrolling through a long list wastes time. Search by booking number, or filter by status: All, In progress, Pending, Archive. Find the booking that needs attention and skip the rest.

**FAQ: How do I look up a specific booking?** Type its number into the search field inside the Bookings section and the matching shipment comes right up.

## Step 3. Open a Booking's Full Details

Tap a card and the short snapshot opens up into the full booking workspace on mobile. The expanded view groups everything a coordinator needs: a map tracing the route, the identifying details (booking number, origin, destination, current status, container type), operational markers (service icons, related profiles, carrier information), the freight amount, and tabs for Details, Tracking, Documents, and Finance.

This is the screen that matters most for day-to-day shipment coordination. A coordinator can get the full shipment context in one place, without pulling up three other systems to check it. The map showing the shipment's path between origin and destination sits right on that same screen.

**FAQ: Is a shipment's route visible inside a mobile freight booking app?** It is. A map on the details screen traces the path between where a shipment starts and where it's headed.

## Step 4. Cargo Details, Terms, and the Charges That Are Easy to Miss

The Details tab holds the substance: what's being shipped and how much it weighs per container, any services requested, comments on file, the conditions attached to the booking, and a product description. Insurance requests show up here too, along with rate terms tied to the shipment mode (door, port, station, or airport at either end) and any additional charges attached to them.

That can mean fees for cancelling a booking, local tax charges, costs tied to currency conversion, commissions from banks, one-off or unexpected charges, surcharges specific to dry ports, and the general Terms & Conditions text. These notes live inside the booking itself, so nobody has to dig through a separate document to find them. A coordinator checking cargo requirements before calling a customer, carrier, or agent gets what's needed on one screen, cutting down on the back-and-forth that usually follows.

**FAQ: Where do I find cargo information for a booking on mobile?** Open the Details tab on a booking's card. It covers the booking conditions, comments left on file, requested services, and cargo specifics down to weight per container.

## Step 5. Timeline

Timeline is where real-time shipment visibility for a booking lives. When a booking is created, that event shows up here, and the log keeps growing as the shipment moves forward, giving the team a quick reference for checking what stage things are at.

## Step 6. Parties and Contacts

Parties & Contacts keeps everyone tied to the booking visible: Platform Owner, Booker, Rate Owner, and other operational profiles connected to the shipment. Each contact card lists who they are, which company or profile they belong to, how to reach them by email or phone, their role, and a Show button for more detail. A new profile can be added straight from the booking whenever a fresh contact needs to be added to the shipment.

**FAQ: Where can I see who's connected to a shipment's booking?** Open Parties & Contacts. It lists everyone by role: booker, platform owner, rate owner, whoever's on support, and any other operational contact.

## Step 7. Add Transport Details on the Spot

Transport details can go into the mobile app the moment they come in: who's hauling it and their company, a phone number to reach them, the transport's own name and its ID numbers, the route itself, and the container number. Container and transport data stay attached to the booking right away, no need to wait until someone's back at a desktop to update the record.

## Step 8. Remarks, Tracking, Documents, and Finance

The Remark field lets the team attach operational notes directly to a booking, keeping shipment communication cleaner and making sure important details stay tied to the right record.

Three more tabs sit inside the booking page. Tracking follows a shipment's progress and any booking-related movement. Documents keeps files tied to the right shipment. Finance covers charges and payments. Future articles will go deeper into each of these tabs and how they support logistics teams tracking shipment updates, documents, and financial standing.

**FAQ: What kind of user relies on a mobile booking management app?** This covers freight forwarders and logistics providers, the coordinators running shipments day to day, support and carrier teams, plus shippers and other partners who need booking information without being tied to a desktop.
