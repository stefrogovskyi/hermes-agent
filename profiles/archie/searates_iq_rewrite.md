A shipper comes to you with cargo that has no business fitting into a rate table. Odd origin, negotiated terms, a fee structure their last forwarder agreed to over three phone calls. Standard tariffs choke on that kind of request. Individual Quotes (IQ) on SeaRates exists for it: a system where you assemble routes, transport modes, services, and fees into one structured offer, calculated automatically.

## What the system actually does

Freight businesses run into non-standard requirements constantly, which means client-specific pricing becomes the norm rather than the exception. IQ lets you build quotations around specific shipment routes and points, assign freight rates and service fees at each stage, and pick from three pricing models: per kg, per lot, or lump sum. You define commercial terms and validity, the platform calculates the total shipment cost for you, and the finished quote goes straight to your customer.

Negotiated rates, confidential pricing, project cargo with its own logic. All of it fits here in a way standard tariffs can't manage.

Who gets the most out of it? Forwarders handling custom client requests, logistics companies selling negotiated rates, carriers moving project or special cargo, multimodal transportation managers, and sales teams preparing tailored offers.

## Creating a quote, step by step

**Step 1. Open Individual Quotes.** Log in to your SeaRates account and go to your Virtual Office Profile. The Individual Quote tab sits in the left-hand menu. Click "Create a tariff" (or "New tariff" if you've built quotes before).

**Step 2. Add the Start point.** This is where the shipment begins. Required fields: location (port or city of origin), shipping type (ocean, road, rail, or air; the form adjusts to whichever you pick), cargo parameters such as container type or weight, and estimated transit time. Optional: shipping line and special cargo requirements.

**Steps 3–4. Add freight rates and delivery fees.** Once the point is saved, add your costs. For ocean freight that might mean a rate per container plus a Bill of Lading fee per shipment. Save to confirm the Start point, or keep going through "Fees for this point" / "Fees for this delivery" to add extra charges. Pricing models map neatly to real situations: per kg suits LCL, per lot covers fixed services, lump sum handles project cargo or negotiated deals. You can invent your own fees too, useful when cost allocation needs to be precise.

**Step 5. Add the End point.** Choose the destination location, then attach destination-side fees: terminal handling, documentation, delivery services, whatever applies. Same three pricing models available. Save the destination point.

**Step 6. Add intermediate points (optional).** There's a "+" icon on the route timeline. Click it to insert intermediate stops or services, each with its own fees and parameters. Transshipment legs and multimodal shipping routes are where this earns its keep.

**Step 7. Add tariff details and terms.** Open Additional Info and set the validity period, tariff name, and commercial terms and conditions. Extras like guarantees, demurrage rates, and storage fees can be added here, and you're free to create new terms if the defaults don't cover your agreement.

**Finally, review.** Check the automatically calculated Shipment Total, confirm every route point and fee, click Save. Done. The quote stays editable, lives on a quote card, and can be shared with customers and partners. What they receive includes the custom pricing, full route visibility, clear transit time, a rate breakdown, transparent fees, and a booking path that takes two clicks.
